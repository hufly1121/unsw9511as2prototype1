Stage 1: Contact the Business Directly

▲

Always start by contacting the business directly. Most disputes are resolved quickly and informally at this stage. This is also a legal requirement before some tribunals will hear your case.

Contact customer service — phone, email, or in person. Explain the problem and what you want.

Follow up in writing — send an email or letter referencing Australian Consumer Law. Keep a copy.

Give a deadline — allow 14 days for a response before escalating.

If Stage 1 Fails

Stage 2: State Fair Trading or Consumer Affairs

▼

If the business refuses to help, your next step is to contact the Fair Trading or Consumer Affairs office in your state or territory. They offer free mediation between you and the business.

australia.gov.au

Lodge an online complaint on your state office's website — most have an online form.

Provide all documentation — receipts, photos, letters, and a timeline of events.

The office will contact the business on your behalf and attempt to negotiate a resolution.

⚖
Last Resort

Stage 3: Civil Tribunal or ACCC

▼

If the dispute is still unresolved after Stage 2, you have two formal options: apply to a Civil and Administrative Tribunal for a binding decision, or report serious misconduct to the ACCC.

Option A: Civil Tribunal

sacat.sa.gov.au

Option B: Report to the ACCC

🇦🇺 Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC)

The ACCC enforces Australian Consumer Law. They do not resolve individual disputes, but reporting your case can trigger investigations into businesses that repeatedly break the law.

accc.gov.au/consumers/consumer-rights-guarantees
⚠️
Know before you apply to a tribunal

Tribunal applications involve a small filing fee and the business may have legal representation. Consider seeking free legal advice from your state's Legal Aid service before proceeding.
