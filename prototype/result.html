✅ Result
🏛 Based on Australian Consumer Law ↗
You have strong consumer rights in this situation.

Under Australian Consumer Law (ACL), when a product has a major failure, you are entitled to choose your preferred remedy. Because the seller has refused your request, you have the right to escalate this complaint.

  • Right to a full refund — You can demand a full refund when a product has a major failure, even if the seller disagrees.
  • Right to a replacement — If you prefer, you can ask for a replacement product of the same type and quality.
  • Right to compensation — If you suffered additional loss due to the faulty product, you may be entitled to compensation on top of a remedy.
  • Cannot be limited by store policy — "No refund" signs and policies do not override your rights under Australian Consumer Law.

This result is based on three things you told us:

  • Recent purchase — Products bought within 30 days carry the strongest consumer protections under ACL.
  • Major failure — A product that stops working or is defective qualifies as a major failure, entitling you to choose your own remedy.
  • Seller refused — Once a business refuses a valid remedy request, you have the right to escalate to Fair Trading or a tribunal.

Australian Consumer Law is federal law and applies equally in every state and territory.

📖 What counts as a "major failure"? ▼

Under Australian Consumer Law, a product has a major failure if:

  • A reasonable person would not have bought it if they knew about the problem
  • It is significantly different from the description or sample
  • It is substantially unfit for its purpose and cannot easily be fixed
  • It is unsafe

Source: Competition and Consumer Act 2010, Schedule 2, Section 260

🏛 Legal basis for these rights ▼

Your rights come from the following legislation:

  • Australian Consumer Law (ACL) — Consumer guarantees for goods: Sections 51–64
  • Remedy rights: Sections 259–267 — Right to repair, replacement, or refund
  • No exclusion of consumer guarantees — Section 64A: Any term purporting to limit or exclude ACL rights is void
