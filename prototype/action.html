Your Action Plan

Follow these steps in order. Start with Step 1 — many issues are resolved without needing to escalate further.

✅
Your rights recap

You have the right to a full refund or replacement for a faulty product under Australian Consumer Law. The seller cannot legally refuse this.

← View full rights summary
1
Start Here

Contact the Seller Directly

The first step is always to contact the business where you bought the product. Explain the problem clearly and state that you are requesting a remedy under Australian Consumer Law. Keep a record of all communication.

💡 Tips for contacting the seller ▼
  • Put your complaint in writing (email or letter) — this creates a paper trail
  • Be clear and polite — state the problem, the remedy you want, and a deadline (e.g. 14 days)
  • Mention Australian Consumer Law specifically in your message
  • Keep all receipts, order confirmations, and product photos
  • Note the name of anyone you speak to and the date of the conversation

2
If Step 1 fails

Contact Your State's Fair Trading Office

If the seller still refuses to help after your written complaint, contact the Fair Trading (or Consumer Affairs) office in your state or territory. They can mediate the dispute for free.

🏛 Fair Trading offices by state ▼
  • NSW: NSW Fair Trading — fairtrading.nsw.gov.au
  • VIC: Consumer Affairs Victoria — consumer.vic.gov.au
  • QLD: Office of Fair Trading — fairtrading.qld.gov.au
  • SA: Consumer and Business Services — cbs.sa.gov.au
  • WA: Consumer Protection — commerce.wa.gov.au
  • TAS: Consumer, Building and Occupational Services — cbos.tas.gov.au
  • ACT: Access Canberra — accesscanberra.act.gov.au
  • NT: Consumer Affairs — consumeraffairs.nt.gov.au

3
Last Resort

Apply to a Tribunal or Contact the ACCC

If the dispute is still unresolved, you can apply to your state's Civil and Administrative Tribunal (CAT) for a formal hearing. Alternatively, for systemic business misconduct, report to the ACCC.

⚖ Tribunal options by state ▼
  • NSW: NSW Civil and Administrative Tribunal (NCAT)
  • VIC: Victorian Civil and Administrative Tribunal (VCAT)
  • QLD: Queensland Civil and Administrative Tribunal (QCAT)
  • SA: South Australian Civil and Administrative Tribunal (SACAT)
  • WA: Magistrates Court of Western Australia (Consumer Protection jurisdiction)
  • All states: ACCC (for reporting, not individual disputes) — accc.gov.au
ℹ️

Tribunal applications usually have a small filing fee. Many hearings can be conducted online or over the phone.
